What we actually pin: the five effects

Layers and layersToo many colours and pens to fit on one wheel — so don't be afraid of space. The container unpacks layer by layer, drawers sliding out like at home; the drawer faces are painted with the chosen effect. The opened containerWhy spend all the space on one circle? An opened container is a space of circles: the wheel steps aside and its contents come out as their own circles beside it — colours, deeper effects, deeper colours, one more circle if you ask. The first wheelA colour effects wheel, not a colour wheel: the ten effects are the wedges, each holding its colours attached. Tap an effect and its colours unfurl round the rim — unpacking happens here, in the menu; on the studio page the wheel stays packed. The five effectsMarker bleed, pastel grain, metallic sheen, watercolour wash, ink hatching — in three colours each. Where the effects law began. Kinds of penPencil, ink, watercolour, paint, marker, pastel, metallic — why menus split into type of pen, colour, effects. New: pencil and paint joined the set. The layered containerThe colour effect dial in a watch-style case — pull it apart and deeper trays slide out: more effects one layer down (charcoal, spray, chalk), more colours below that. The container is empty space, as layered as you build it. The colour effect dialNot a colour menu, not a pen menu — one dial of colour effects. Turn either ring and the other re-inks itself; the lens is the part. Menus, combinedTwo dials, one part: the outer dial turns the colour, the inner dial turns the pen, the lens shows the effect live. The digital-space answer to the corner chip. Colours in the containerThe container arrives with your most-used colours; each colour brings all five of its effects. Pick your own, drag parts round inside.
